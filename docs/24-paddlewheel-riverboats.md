# Paddlewheel Heritage and the Riverboat Industry

## Established canon
Paddlewheel riverboats are a **thriving tourism attraction of Valle de Santa Aurelia**, alongside maritime museums, grand ballrooms, historic timber-and-stone boardwalks, weddings, and the sea-going cruise trade. The local fleet serves sightseeing, dinner cruises, music, heritage programming and private events. Vessel names, operators and exact routes below are **working proposals**, not fixed canon.

## The nineteenth-century river
Indigenous estuary communities had rich precolonial canoe, trade and pilotage traditions; Spanish and other colonial sailing/rowing craft entered the estuary in 1608. **Steam-powered paddleboats belong to the nineteenth century, never the founding fleet.**

Proposed timeline: 1850s–1870s paddle steamers appear on the lower Columbia; 1870s–1910s sternwheelers connect river settlements, materials, timber and mining traffic where the waterways actually permit it. Road and rail then displace the freight business, but a heritage excursion fleet survives or is revived during the twentieth century. Historic hulls are carefully distinguished from replicas.

## Modern fleet concepts
- **PS Reina de las Dos Orillas** (working name), a modern historic-style large sternwheeler, with covered dining saloon and open observation decks. Main estuary and Columbia sightseeing, sunset meals, music and wedding charters. Its wheel should genuinely propel the boat if represented as such; otherwise declare that it is decorative and another engine drives propellers.
- **PS La Estrella del Río**, a smaller restored or faithful replica working sternwheel steamer for history demonstrations, school visits and shorter runs. Historic authenticity versus modern engines/boiler is accurately labeled.
- **PS Willowwick Belle**, a compact vessel for a *specifically surveyed and sufficiently deep, wide and sheltered inland reach*. Its name does not magically make inland Willowwick navigable from the Columbia.
- Smaller harbor launches, water taxis and workboats are **not** described as paddlewheelers merely because they serve the same waterfront.

## Experiences and routes
**Historic Harbor Loop:** Santa Aurelia waterfront, the preserved founding flagship, Spanish and Venetian palace facades, commercial working docks and stone-and-timber promenades, with estuary views.

**Lower Columbia Day Cruise:** a longer route in approved river channels, past forested shores, maritime industry and bird habitats, potentially connecting suitable piers only if actual river geography permits.

**Two Shores Dinner Sail:** music, local food, evening light and indoor protection from Pacific Northwest rain, cold and wind. Private weddings, rehearsal dinners and anniversary parties support the [Wedding Coast](23-wedding-coast-economy-and-waterfront.md).

**Festival Sailings:** ticketed river cruises and supervised decorated flotillas during the Festival of the Two Hearts. Maintain commercial shipping lanes and realistic port controls.

**Heritage Education:** paddle mechanics, the change from Indigenous waterways to colonial sail and industrial river transport, conservation of vessels, and the contributions of sailors and shipwrights from Spanish America, Europe and the Philippines.

## Working shore-side economy
Riverboat operators support pier staff, captains, engineers, mechanics, licensed deck crews, performers, caterers, shipwrights, historical interpreters, event planners and nearby lodging. Some venues may coordinate with the larger cruise ship terminal, but **ocean-going cruise ship turnarounds must operate in deep-water areas**, separate from shallow excursion docks. The fleet should coexist with the working fishing, cargo and maritime industries.

## Navigation limits
The Columbia is a tidal, wind-affected commercial navigation system, and the hazardous Columbia Bar stands between the estuary and the open Pacific. Paddlewheel excursion vessels operate on approved **protected river routes**, not routine ocean crossings. Larger vessels need appropriate berth draft, bridge clearance, weather policies, trained pilots, accessible boarding and modern safety compliance. The route from Santa Aurelia to Willowwick is **not presumed navigable**; it must be established in our geographic/hydrological map before through-cruises are canonized.

See also [the ocean cruise homeport](25-santa-aurelia-cruise-homeport.md). The industries overlap through hotels, transport, waterfront attractions and pre-/post-cruise excursions but remain technically distinct.

## Maritime rescue coordination
The nearby **Coast Guard base outside Santa Aurelia** reinforces the valley's professional maritime response capabilities. Licensed riverboat masters remain responsible for vessel operations and weather decisions, while Coast Guard resources respond to emergencies and applicable maritime regulations.
