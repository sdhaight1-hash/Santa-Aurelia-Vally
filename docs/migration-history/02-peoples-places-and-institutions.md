# Peoples, Places, Migration Corridors and Lasting Institutions

This is a **relational registry**, not an ethnic zoning map. A group's presence is established or proposed independently from its exact date or size. People's identities persist across marriages, institutions, moves, generations and more than one settlement.

## Why people moved: five overlapping mechanisms
1. **Family and marriage chains:** relatives sponsor arrivals and encourage moves to already-known valleys, extending kinship and support networks across migration periods.
2. **Work and skill networks:** maritime shipping and canning; mining and timber; cattle and horse breeding; stonework; horticulture; salons and arts; academic, medical and government jobs.
3. **Investment and property:** wealthy merchant capital, mining syndicates, estate acquisitions, land claims, and later inheritance—unequal to wage migrants' ability to obtain property.
4. **Refuge, ideology and community:** religious congregations, mutual-aid societies, working-class associations, 1970s back-to-land motives, forest conservation.
5. **Compulsion and constraint:** epidemics, land displacement, debt, labor contracting, exclusion laws, military mobilization, war, climate shocks and industrial closures. **Not all movement is voluntary.**

## Distinct city and district identities

### Santa Aurelia — old Pacific city, maritime internationalism
Spanish-Indigenous and New Spanish/Philippine founding networks form the earliest mixed community. Venetian merchants and artisans later reshape its architecture, harbor banking and formal ballroom season. Chinese, Scandinavian/Finnish and other working waterfront populations contribute essential labor and ordinary neighborhoods. The city is **both affluent and made by working people**. Its oldest families do not automatically outrank sovereign Indigenous institutions.

### Willowwick Grove — inland mining, eastern capital and gardens
Gold and related mining draw more than investors; laboring households create the town's foundations. Connecticut Ashcroft finance and Virginia Blackthorn estate/timber wealth are prominent. Whitcomb industry, Bellamy education and Vale arts define the broader elite roster, but their individual founders' home states and genealogies still require verification in-world. Japanese/Korean horticulture, Chinese businesses, Slavic artisans and other communities shape the city as profoundly as wealthy philanthropy.

### Blackwood Ridge — many histories, one uncanny landscape
Indigenous stories and woodland governance precede immigrant arrivals. Irish, Scots, Romanian/Transylvanian, Polish, Czech, Slovak and other families form practical homesteads and villages at accessible margins. Their old-world beliefs meet—but do not replace—existing stories of stones, luminous fungi, White Ones and forest sounds. Working logging access repeatedly fails deep within Witches' Wood, preserving old-growth while other ridges have ordinary forestry. Later Fernhollow ecological research is an additional layer, not an original civilizing act.

### Cattle country — South Hills and suitable upper valleys
Earlier Spanish-influenced ranching, local mixed farms and later migrants from Wyoming form an overlapping ranch community. The **Wyoming influx [E]** may be set around **1887–1915 [W]**. Strong 4-H and rodeo cultures grow later as civic institutions. Land limitations and wet-climate adaptation matter.

### Smaller townships
Southbank (separate mill community), Duskwood (independent eastern forest-edge settlement), Fernhollow (older rural country plus modern ecovillage), Thornhaven (modern living-history project), and proposed Blackwood villages have **different histories**. Do not invent founding dates or assume their current residents all descend from one surname.

## Institutions to chronicle across generations
- **Port and founding household archives:** voyage registers, shipping contracts, marriage records and Sáweli's authored manuscripts; private Valcárcel archive is not a public census bureau.
- **Tribal governing institutions:** ancestral authority, sovereign government, land history, archives and right to control sensitive records; do not equate reservation with a single ethnic neighborhood.
- **Faith communities:** old Catholic port life; later Protestant, Eastern Orthodox and other congregations; changing membership and ecumenical/interfaith history.
- **Craft and economic institutions:** guild-like professional associations (names U), canneries, fishermen's lodges, maritime unions, mining companies, millworker networks, farm cooperatives, saddle/bow workshops.
- **Social institutions:** Venice-inspired ballroom circuit, garden-city conservancy, immigrant halls, libraries, schools, clubs, 4-H, FFA, local rodeo committees, festivals and food markets.
- **Intergenerational changes:** language shift, bilingual homes, marriage between communities, suburbanization, education, return migration, class mobility, and heritage celebrations.

## Making a believable migration household (template)
Each new historical household should have: **who they were; home town/region; movement year or approximate interval; route/transport; reason; companions and kin; land/occupation; arrival destination; language/faith if relevant and not stereotyped; descendants or departure; documentary evidence; impact/conflicts; status label.**

Avoid inventing entire family dynasties merely to fill every grid cell. A few deeply researched households do more worldbuilding than 100 shallow surnames.

## Political-era implications for settlement history
The [Long Transfer](../37-political-succession-1608-1859.md) now establishes **U.S. sovereignty succession in early 1821** over the old Spanish-origin port, with continuity of existing municipal and family networks. Venetian commercial migration c.1815–1855 therefore overlaps late Spanish, transitional and American-administered years. Connecticut/Virginia immigration into Willowwick's mining economy largely comes later. Newly American administration does **not** itself establish exact ethnic shares, erase Spanish or Indigenous populations, validate colonial title claims or settle the reservation's history.
