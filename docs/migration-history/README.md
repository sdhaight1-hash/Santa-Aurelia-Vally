## Historical premises that cannot be undone
- Indigenous people and nations **already lived in and governed** this region before 1608. Their descendants are modern peoples, not a vanished introductory chapter. The fictional Ashalewan and Blackwood-associated communities require careful definition; historical Clatsop/Chinookan peoples are real-world analogues, not automatically interchangeable with invented groups.
- The permanent European-founded port at the real Astoria location begins **1608**, with **67 documented people in two ships (48 + 19)** from the *invented* founding registers—not 67 ethnic Spaniards. Their origins include Iberia, New Spain, Indigenous communities of the Americas, the Philippines, other Pacific/Atlantic trading networks and a Genoese merchant factor. The surrounding Indigenous population was already present.
- The Alonso–Sáweli/Inés marriage is **c.1611 working**, with **nine children** canonical. Her independently authored historical work and Indigenous knowledge networks precede later migrant folklore.
- The real-world **1811 founding of Fort Astoria cannot recur unchanged** in a place already occupied by a centuries-old Spanish-founded port. British, American and Spanish institutional succession requires an explicit alternate timeline.
- The **1700 Cascadia event** remains a major rupture: fatalities, relocation, rebuilding, displaced households, property disputes and archive losses are consequences, not just aesthetic reconstruction. The port persists.
- The nineteenth-century **Venetian commercial/architectural migration belongs especially to Santa Aurelia**, whereas the **Connecticut/Virginia mining, investment and civic-elite migration belongs principally to Willowwick Grove**.
- Irish, Scottish, Romanian/Transylvanian and Slavic settlements are especially prominent in Blackwood Ridge and its outer valleys, but **do not displace earlier Indigenous knowledge or imply prehistoric Celtic settlement is factual**.
- Additional recognized populations include Scandinavians/Finns, Chinese, Japanese, Koreans, Filipinos, Central/Eastern Europeans, later ranching families from **Wyoming**, and ongoing twentieth–twenty-first-century migrants. Do not reduce any population to a decorative craft or ethnic-only village.
- The **Wyoming rancher migration itself is user-established**; the proposed c.1887–1915 wave and specific motives remain adjustable.
- **Fernhollow ecovillage** forms in the **1970s** within a district with older settlements. **Duskwood Township** is a separate forest-edge community with an as-yet undecided founding date.
- The old-growth ecology, traditional foraging and varied legal titles in Witches' Wood preclude making it an empty agricultural or migrant homesteading frontier.

## Evidence labels (use in every migration-history chapter)
**E — Established by user / locked narrative:** a fact the setting must preserve.
**W — Working in-world reconstruction:** plausible proposed year, wave, motive, local scene or family whose exact form remains adjustable.
**R — External real-world historical reference:** documented evidence about real Oregon, the Columbia, Wyoming or migration law; it never by itself proves the alternate-history event.
**U — Unresolved:** requires later geographic, demographic, political or archival choice.

For citations to real history and existing world-bible sources see [Evidence Register](04-evidence-and-open-decisions.md). **These are not real-world genealogical, census or immigration records for a Spanish-founded Astoria.**

## Editorial rule
Migration changes labor, food, class, landholding, institutions, language, housing, faith and relations with earlier inhabitants. Avoid **one-wave-one-ethnicity-one-town** logic. Follow children and grandchildren, internal relocation, mixed marriages, arrivals and departures, circular movement and coerced migration. A wave is a period of relatively notable movement, not proof that everybody arrived then.

## Political succession now established
The principal sovereignty gap has been resolved in the [Long Transfer political-history chapter](../37-political-succession-1608-1859.md): **early 1821** negotiated transfer of Spain's asserted sovereignty over the established city to U.S. administration, with limited charter/property continuity; **1846** Oregon Treaty boundary consolidation; **1848** territorial incorporation; and **1859** statehood. Tribal sovereignty, precise land titles, and details of the invented diplomatic annex remain unresolved. This supersedes older statements in this index describing the *overall transition* as undetermined.
