# Santa Aurelia Cruise Homeport — The Ocean Gateway

## Established alternate-history canon

Modern **Santa Aurelia is both a sea-going cruise departure port (homeport) and a port of call**, significantly more developed as a passenger embarkation center than present-day real Astoria, Oregon. Passengers can **begin and end ocean cruises here**, not merely disembark for a few hours during a stop. The city's cruise sector, paddlewheel excursions, historic waterfront, Wedding Coast, and Festival of the Two Hearts form interconnected but operationally distinct businesses.

**Plausibility distinction:** in our real world, **Astoria is primarily a cruise port of call, not an established homeport for regular embarking/disembarking passengers**. The fictional Santa Aurelia diverges in decades of infrastructure investment, transport links and passenger operations. The actual Port of Astoria Pier 1 is a deep-draft cruise berth; it is a useful **geographic precedent, not proof that the homeport infrastructure already exists**.

## The harbor layout

Proposed modern waterfront has **three separate operating districts**, minimizing conflict with the heritage core:

1. **Ocean Passenger Terminal**, a dedicated large-vessel embarkation complex in a deep-water pier zone near—but not inside—the most fragile historic waterfront. It accommodates passenger handling and selected cruise turnaround operations as well as ordinary port-of-call calls. Its exact site, footprint, draft and maximum ship size must be surveyed and mapped rather than asserted.
2. **Historic Harbor and Excursion Piers**, close to the timber-and-stone boardwalks, the preserved founding flagship, dinner-cruise docks, and the grand Spanish and Venetian palazzi. Medium and small excursions and riverboat visitors use this district; large ocean ships don't moor against historic shallow terraces.
3. **Commercial Marine and Service Area**, for fishing fleets, cargo, tug and pilot support, vessel repairs, stores, waste management, shore power/utilities and other working-port needs. Passenger growth should not displace the entire commercial maritime economy.

Passengers may transfer by well-managed shuttle, pedestrian connections where safe, accessible waterfront transport, or small local ferries to approved docks; **a paddlewheel boat is not interchangeable with a big cruise ship tender or port shuttle**.

## Why homeporting developed here

Santa Aurelia's older port wealth, exceptional harbor identity, tourist architecture, preserved founding vessels, competing ballroom estates and destination-wedding reputation made the town a powerful regional draw. Its modern government and maritime operators invested far more in passenger operations than the corresponding real-world Astoria.

**Working historical development:** initial ocean cruise port-of-call business becomes notable in the later twentieth century; a new or enlarged terminal and shore-side turnaround infrastructure attract limited homeport departures; subsequent investment in transport, lodging and services allows a **regular seasonal homeport program**. Do not assume enormous year-round megaship operations merely because cruise liners can dock.

## The turnaround problem: what a real homeport needs

Unlike a port-of-call, a homeport must manage **thousands of passengers and luggage in two directions on the same day**. Facilities include:
- secure passenger check-in, ticketing, screening, baggage sorting and reclaim;
- applicable border, immigration, customs and public-health arrangements for international voyages;
- gangways, accessible circulation, waiting spaces, controlled traffic and emergency evacuation;
- coach and shuttle staging, accessible transport, guest parking and road capacity;
- provision loading, food/catering supply, drinking water, waste and sewage reception, stores and laundry;
- crew transfers, hotel rooms, medical services, hotels for pre/post-cruise nights, and schedule recovery during delays;
- berth reservation coordination with pilots, tugs, Coast Guard, commercial ships and weather services;
- shore-power and emissions controls where technically available, with noise and coastal impacts managed.

**A homeport here is more economically consequential than a berth for visiting ships.** Some visitors stay several nights before departure and after return. They fill inns and restaurants, shop with local craftspeople, take paddlewheel tours, visit the family-history museums and book scenic outings. It also creates congestion, housing and seasonal workforce pressures.

## Pacific cruise itineraries (working concepts)

**Pacific Coast Voyages:** Santa Aurelia departures calling at appropriate Washington, British Columbia and California ports, with legally valid itineraries for the flags and passenger-vessel rules involved.

**Alaska Voyages:** seasonal departures northward with suitable Canadian/international port arrangements, operating primarily in the better-weather cruise season. Exact sailing durations and commercial availability are not yet fixed.

**North Pacific Scenic and Heritage Voyages:** smaller premium ships departing Santa Aurelia for coastal destinations with adequate infrastructure, weather windows and customs arrangements.

**Seasonal repositioning:** departures or arrivals connecting to major Pacific coast ports, rather than claiming that every large vessel sails round-trip all year.

**US passenger-cabotage caution:** passenger-vessel rules for foreign-flag ships can constrain coastwise itineraries and require foreign ports. No fictional local law automatically overrides federal maritime law; choose plausible itineraries and flags when detailing individual schedules.

## The Columbia Bar is the gatekeeper

Ships depart the **lower Columbia estuary**, not a calm open-water Mediterranean harbor. The dangerous **Columbia River Bar**, shifting weather, tides, currents, river traffic, pilots, tugs and potential seasonal closures constrain vessel movements. A ship's ability to berth at Santa Aurelia does not guarantee it can cross the bar on a given day; ship class, sea state, port restrictions and professional judgment matter.

Cruise departures are advertised with contingency plans: missed calls, late returns, delayed boarding, cancellation, and reserve time. Serious operators avoid promising constant reliable crossings through dangerous conditions.

## The city and regional economy

Santa Aurelia is now both:
- **A departure and arrival gateway:** hotel nights, luggage, port transfers, parking, provisioning, crew and support services.
- **A destination in its own right:** visitors seeking the timber-and-stone waterfront, Spanish/Venetian architecture, [Season of Halls](22-season-of-halls-venetian-ballrooms.md), [Wedding Coast](23-wedding-coast-economy-and-waterfront.md), [paddlewheel riverboats](24-paddlewheel-riverboats.md), local gardens, culture and maritime collections.

The adjacent valley receives demand for flowers, pastries, catering, wine, logistics, excursion guides, transport, inns, food producers, skilled repairs and arts. The port is not exclusively a cruise attraction, nor should cruise marketing overwhelm resident access to the public boardwalk.

## Relationship to the Two Hearts Festival and wedding trade

The **Festival of the Two Hearts** could attract themed visiting-ship calls, ceremonial departures and special charter sailings, especially with the historic palaces illuminated along the waterfront. A couple might hold their ceremony in the Palacio, spend a weekend enjoying the Season of Halls, then depart on a honeymoon cruise from Santa Aurelia. Such linkages are attractive but do not mean the festival, cruise terminal and wedding venues are operated as one monopoly.

## Real-world grounding and divergence

- **Port of Astoria, Pier 1:** real cruise berth, 1,100-foot north face and deep-water facilities (official port source): https://portofastoria.specialdistrict.org/pier-1
- **Port of Astoria general cruise information:** deep-water port near the Columbia mouth, existing port-of-call infrastructure: https://portofastoria.specialdistrict.org/general-cruise-information
- **Princess Cruises, Astoria destination:** explicitly identifies real Astoria as a **port of call rather than a departure port**: https://www.princess.com/en-int/cruise-destinations/california-coast-cruises/cruises-to-astoria-oregon

These real-world facts are supporting geography and contrast, **not claims that a fictional Santa Aurelia homeport exists in real Oregon**.

## Canon and open development

**User-established:** Santa Aurelia is a **cruise ship departure port** as well as a visiting port; river paddlewheel tourism thrives; historic boardwalks, grand halls, weddings and a coast/valley tourism economy reinforce one another.

**Working:** the terminal name, physical location and footprint, phase-by-phase homeport investment history, number of simultaneous turnarounds, specific cruise brands and ship names, homeport passenger counts, cruise seasons, routes and transport providers. Do not invent confirmed services or real-world passenger statistics for the fictional city. Future world maps must connect passenger terminals, heritage docks, commercial ship zones and inland transport realistically.

## Coast Guard support nearby
A substantial **Coast Guard base outside the city**, with naval/maritime facilities, supports Columbia Bar emergency response, maritime safety, law enforcement and search-and-rescue coordination affecting the homeport. It is **not part of the cruise terminal**, and the Coast Guard does not replace licensed pilots, the ship's master or local port operators. See [the nearby base](26-coast-guard-base-santa-aurelia.md).

## Joint naval installation near the homeport
**Updated canon:** the nearby facility is a **joint U.S. Coast Guard–U.S. Navy base** outside Santa Aurelia. The Coast Guard retains its distinct maritime safety and rescue role for the port. The Navy separately maintains naval facilities and operational readiness activities; its presence does not make the civilian cruise terminal a naval facility. See [joint base](26-coast-guard-base-santa-aurelia.md).
