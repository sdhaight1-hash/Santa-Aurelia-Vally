# Canon, degrees of certainty, and editorial rules

## Core agreements made explicitly by the user
The setting is **Valle de Santa Aurelia**, a broader regional world centered on multiple communities, **not** simply Willowwick Grove. **Santa Aurelia** is the oldest permanent European-founded settlement, established **1608** at present-day **Astoria, Oregon**; Spanish colonial maritime history and lasting cooperative relations with local Indigenous peoples are fundamental. The founding captain is wealthy in Spanish gold and has a **definite offshoot of Spanish royal ancestry**. His eventual marriage to the **adult daughter of a local chief** is a deeply loving mutual relationship, not principally a political arrangement. They are remembered across centuries as a defining local romance; numerous other settler–Indigenous marriages occurred. Their bloodline has descendants today.

Santa Aurelia is **“The Jewel of the Pacific”** and shows a distinctive **Spanish–Indigenous architectural synthesis**, subsequently shaped by a substantial arrival of **Venetian Italian migrants, skilled artisans, and merchant wealth**. Its historic boardwalk festival celebrating the couple culminates in a prestigious debutante ball. The *underlying annual formal social assembly* is a tradition extending to the **seventeenth-century colony**, though its present debutante format evolved later. The waterfront ball hall began in the **1600s** and grew by successive expansions. The captain's **original flagship** survives as a carefully restored harbor attraction, with material replacement and preservation needed for realism.

Santa Aurelia is **almost as populous as Willowwick Grove**. The nineteenth-century **Connecticut and Virginia migrations, finance, and old-money dynasties belong primarily to Willowwick**, **not Santa Aurelia**. Willowwick was **originally a mining settlement**, with **gold** the principal initial metal, plus copper and quarrying. The regional geology has been explicitly altered to include a **cratonic tectonic element**.

**Witches' Wood**, on Blackwood Ridge, possesses a prehistoric stone circle and mounds; mysterious *waystones* also occur valleywide. The ring exists in the same habitat as the enormous endemic **Blacksilver Willow**, with dark near-black bark, silvery leaves, giant weeping crown, and unique global distribution. Bioluminescent fungi associate closely with the trees. The ring's popular name is **the Fairy Stones**; **the Giants' Ring** is an alternative linked to ancestral legends. Fog, vines, trees, and fungi contribute to the atmosphere. Legends encompass the **White Ones / giants**, unusual putative ancestral Celtic/Pictish connection in one Indigenous community, will-o'-the-wisps, children following lights and disappearing, Slavic/Romanian parallels, werewolves, Bigfoot, and Missing 411-style interpretations of disappearances. All remain naturalistically framed in the objective setting.

Older **Irish, Scottish, Romanian/Transylvanian, and Slavic** communities concentrate around Witches' Wood; some immigrants describe a strange sense of kinship with the place. **Fernhollow Country** and rural self-reliant/prepper/homesteading communities lie southwest of Witches' Wood. The **reservation** lies northwest and **includes part of Witches' Wood itself**.

## Substantial collaboratively developed directions (strong working canon, details editable)
- Santa Aurelia / Willowwick Grove / greater valley populations ~19k / ~21k / ~60k.
- Original colonial port `Puerto de Santa Aurelia`; historic whole-region name `Valle de Santa Aurelia`, English `Aurelia Valley`.
- The founding captain's proposed name **Don Alonso de Valcárcel y Austria**; proposed 1572–1641. His ancestry was proposed as an **invented additional illegitimate son of Don John of Austria** and therefore grandson of Charles V, first cousin of Philip III. This genealogy is **an alternate-history invention**, not a real documented child. His proposed maternal family background involves the wealthy Valcárcel merchants. His adult Indigenous spouse's **Spanish-recorded name is Inés**; original Indigenous name, community, kinship, and language **remain undecided**.
- **1611** as working marriage year, subject to date-specific confirmation.
- Early negotiated harbor occupancy and intercommunity arrangements, later called the **Covenant of the Two Shores**; proposed **Consejo de las Dos Orillas** as consultative council, without erasing distinct governments.
- **1638** as working construction year for an initial timber-and-stone **Casa de las Asambleas**, later expanded as **Palacio de la Bahía**. Modern debutante form emerges in later centuries.
- **January 26, 1700 Cascadia earthquake and tsunami** damages Santa Aurelia. **Santa Aurelia rebuilds and remains an important living port**; it is *not* abandoned or replaced by Willowwick.
- A flourishing nineteenth-century Venetian merchant migration and architectural movement (suggested 1815–1855 with 1830s–1840s core). Names such as **Riva di Santa Aurelia**, a merchant family **Contarini di Valverde**, and an associated masquerade are **suggested, not irrevocably adopted**.
- Willowwick's historic Victorian beauty, ornamental gardens, crafts, architecture, institutional life, and civic improvement movement; Santa Aurelia's equally independent maritime/Venetian/Spanish-Indigenous identity.
- Witches' Wood's named places and villages (Glenmorrow, Valea Argintie, Starý Brod), ecology, and scientific explanations: most names and individual incidents are **proposals** awaiting review.

## Explicit unresolved matters

- Exact valley dimensions, river network, Willowwick location, relative coordinates, roads and municipal boundaries.
- Degree of wider counterfactual impact of Spanish settlement in 1608: royal permission, Manila ship routes, Spanish-British-American legal transition, property titles.
- Geological model: full craton versus exotic detached cratonic fragment, deposit ages and mineralization systems.
- Extent and provenance of ancient European-related genetics; whether any pre-1608 ancient DNA evidence is confirmed or only tentatively interpreted.
- Archaeological dates and builders of stone circles/mounds/waystones; nomenclature and rights of the Indigenous nation.
- Botanical formal names, confirmed ecological mechanisms, tree lifespan and height, particular fungus taxa.
- Proposed named landmarks and institutions should not be mistaken for explicit user approvals without confirmation.

## Tone and epistemic discipline
Write the region as a functioning society with ordinary neighborhoods, infrastructure, class differences, industry, governance, schools, and cultural disagreements—not a theme park or secretly magical world. Distinguish *invented in-setting primary sources*, *in-setting folkloric claims*, *in-setting science*, and *actual external historical background*. Do not assert there is real pre-Columbian Celtic/Pictish settlement in actual Oregon. Do not portray giant humanoids or portals as objective reality. Never confuse tribal descent with tribal citizenship.


## Founding dynasty revision — explicit lore and reconstruction
The user has established that **Alonso and Inés had nine children** and that four surviving original colonial paintings (arrival, Inés, wedding, full eleven-person family) were painted by a **famous Spanish painter who was Alonso's friend and accompanied the 1608 expedition**. Two generations of family ancestry have now been added to the genealogy. **Alonso’s imperial descent through Don John is counterfactual**, even though Charles V, Barbara Blomberg and Don John were real. **Inés's unnamed maternal and paternal ancestors are editorial placeholders** until her Indigenous nation and naming conventions are developed.
