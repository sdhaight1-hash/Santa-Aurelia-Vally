# Blackwood Ridge communities — reservation, Fernhollow and rural hinterland

## The district
Blackwood Ridge is a lived-in district of historic villages, old forests, tribal lands, isolated farms, logging roads and unconventional communities, **not one forest or single municipality**. Its residents encompass Indigenous families, old Irish/Scottish/Romanian/Slavic lines, outdoorspeople, ordinary commuters, craftspeople, ecologists, preppers, hunters, traditional Christians, pagans, skeptics and others. Mutual assistance in storms and wilderness emergencies is more important than ideological unity.

## Reservation: northwest, including Witches' Wood
The reservation lies on the **northwestern outer approaches of Witches' Wood and extends significantly inside the woodland**. It includes residences in more accessible valleys, forest, streams, ancestral monuments, waystones, culturally important areas and some endemic willow groves. Sovereign tribal governance, land rights and separate cultural interpretation apply. **Cedarwater Casino & Lodge** is on tribally eligible land near an accessible road and commercially integrated with wider region; it is owned by the **tribal government**, not an individual family. The exact nation's name, historic treaties and jurisdictional boundaries await design. The Fairy Stones ring might sit near the boundary, but do **not** assign it to a landowner prematurely.

## Fernhollow Country: southwest
The **broader rural district** southwest of Witches' Wood is called **Fernhollow Country** (working regional term). Includes older immigrant farms/villages, independent homesteads and preparedness households, managed woods, workshops, market farms and more accessible valleys. **Fernhollow ecovillage** is one sub-community within that district, not its whole population.

### Fernhollow ecovillage
Founded during the **1970s back-to-the-land movement** on a rural farm and woodlot; develops into a practical, economically active ecovillage. Timber-framed, carefully weatherproofed cob and small homes; orchards, greenhouses, food forests, market gardens, rainwater and water treatment, managed mushroom cultivation, aquaponics demonstrations, natural construction, bushcraft/foraging workshops and apprenticeships. It operates with real regulations, modern utilities where needed, cooperative decisions, external trade and paid work—no miraculous utopia.

### Homesteaders and preparedness culture
Southwest Blackwood includes people who maintain wells, septic, backup generators/solar, stored water and food, gardens, root cellars and winter access plans in an earthquake/landslide/storm-prone environment. Some are explicitly survivalists or privacy-focused; others are ordinary hunting/rural families. Archery, traditional bowmaking, woodworking, livestock, forestry, food preservation, bushcraft and emergency mutual aid are normal. Politics and faith differ across households. Building permits, fire risks, hunting law and land access create everyday conflicts.

### Thornhaven Village
Separate, limited-population **medieval-style living-history village** within reasonable travel distance of Fernhollow; modern utilities and safety systems disguised behind period aesthetics. Used for Renaissance Faire, LARP, craft courses, archery and living-history education. Not an ancient medieval settlement. Links to regional traditional trades, theater, forestry and Fernhollow ecological building, while maintaining separate governance/business.

## Historic immigrant communities (names proposed, not fixed)
**Glenmorrow:** Irish/Scottish-rooted village near a forest approach, pipers, old chapel, farms, traditional music, fairy stories and craft trade.

**Valea Argintie:** Romanian/Transylvanian-rooted sheltered tributary valley, carved gates, steep-roof homes, Orthodox/Catholic/Protestant life, orchards, woodland crafts, mushroom lore and Blackwood Howler/lantern traditions. The Romanian-language name means roughly Silver Valley.

**Starý Brod:** Czech/Slovak-rooted old ford and stoneway crossing, Central/Eastern European miners, forestry, stonecutting, craft workshops and folk traditions. Other groups present and long-term intermarriage common.

Do not model these villages as ethnic-only ghettos: each has multiple migration layers, people who do not care about folklore, contemporary homes and changing economies.

## The “unexplained kinship” motif
Generations of Irish, Scottish, Romanian and Slavic migrants have described an uncanny sense of belonging near Blackwood Ridge despite its frightening reputation. Residents interpret this as ancestral recognition, spiritual calling, climate similarity or simply mountains/forests reminiscent of home. Chain migration, available land, mining/forestry work, existing community and familiar foggy uplands offer grounded explanations. Their feeling of kinship is genuine experience, **not proven supernatural attraction**.

## Wilderness interface
Settlement is mainly **around** the woodland, not in the most sensitive Fairy Stones/Blacksilver Willow core. Forest use includes tribal cultural access, wildlife management, public recreation where allowed, protected biology, research, working forestry, rescue, hunting and privately held land. Road closures, erosion, storms and unauthorized night tourism remain practical concerns.

### Fernhollow's Witches' Wood ecological research
**Established:** Over decades, the Fernhollow ecovillage has conducted substantial ecological observation of **Witches' Wood** and applied the findings to its own evolving **food-forest design**. Since the 1970s it has developed seasonal foraging records, habitat notes, canopy/light and soil/moisture studies, mushroom-cultivation trials and perennial polyculture experiments. The ancient forest's natural gaps, wetlands, forest layers and fungal systems inform managed productive groves, but the ecovillage **does not claim to recreate old-growth forest or own its resources**. Witches' Wood itself is a long-used, surprisingly productive foraging landscape in suitable permitted areas. Forest-access limitations, sovereign tribal governance and protected sites apply; the ecology is studied under appropriate permission. Repeated failed industrial logging attempts helped extensive ancient old-growth persist, whereas careful small-scale foraging and observation have enduring local traditions. Complete context in [Fernhollow Food-Forest Ecology](33-fernhollow-food-forest-ecology.md).

## Blacksilver Willow woodcraft and regional bowyers
The endemic **Blacksilver Willow** produces an exceptionally prized **ebony-dark heartwood** with unusual mechanical qualities that make selected straight-grained pieces valuable to local **traditional bowmakers**. The same timber is used in fine furniture, cabinetry, carved and inlaid architectural decoration in Willowwick Grove, Santa Aurelia, South Hills and Fernhollow craft traditions. This *does not* mean ancient Silverveil and Witches' Wood groves are available for logging: protected stands and sovereign land rights remain respected, with **most working timber coming from centuries-old cultivated Blacksilver populations outside Witches' Wood**, plus supplementary reclaimed and authorized salvage sources. Cultivated trees usually remain smaller at maturity than native forest giants. Exact mechanical properties and production yields remain under study. See [Blacksilver Willow Woodcraft](34-blacksilver-willow-woodcraft.md).

## Witches' Wood: protected by reputation, designation still contested
**Latest canon:** Much of Witches' Wood's ancient old growth has remained standing **without a comprehensive formally recognized protected-area designation**. Repeated timber-company failures, difficult terrain, local accounts of disasters and the woodland's ominous folklore have discouraged industrial entry for generations. By the present day **most residents avoid disturbing the deeper woods unnecessarily**, though familiar outer paths and legally permissible foraging remain in use.

**Fernhollow ecovillage and the sovereign tribal government with lands in northwestern Witches' Wood are actively advocating for formal, enduring protection.** Their motivations can overlap—old-growth habitat, watershed integrity, cultural sites, and stewardship—but they are separate institutions with distinct authority. A proposed landscape-level conservation framework must respect existing reservation sovereignty, other parcel owners and valid pre-existing protections; it does **not** mean all Witches' Wood currently lacks legal protection or that the tribe needs an outside designation to govern its own land.

The forest's reputation has functioned as a powerful **informal deterrent**, not an enforceable legal guarantee. The precise designation proposal, maps, procedures, supporters, opposition and legal outcome are not yet established. See [Natural History](30-natural-history-geology-climate-ecology.md) and [Fernhollow ecological research](33-fernhollow-food-forest-ecology.md).

## Duskwood Township — eastern forest outskirts
**Duskwood Township** is a newly established **independent small community on the eastern/southeastern outskirts of Witches' Wood**, distinct from southwestern Fernhollow ecovillage and from sovereign northwestern reservation lands. Its **working** location is 45.8750° N, 123.5920° W; its **working** population is roughly 900–1,400, not a confirmed census figure. Duskwood is a practical forest-edge settlement of homes, modest shops, tradespeople, foraging families and outer-trail services, rather than a settlement inside protected ancient groves. It remembers the failed industrial logging ventures but residents also maintain ordinary links to the woodland. Its specific town institutions and precise road routes remain open. See [Duskwood Township](35-duskwood-township.md).

## Ranching and rodeo on the outer ridges
Suitable farmland in South Hills, Fernhollow Country and Duskwood's outskirts supports cattle, horse properties, mixed farms and participating **4-H families**. **Rodeo is a major part of regional culture**, including recurring arena events and youth competitions. Descendants of **Wyoming ranching families who migrated to the valley long ago** help sustain horsemanship and cattle traditions alongside older local ranching families; the proposed migration wave is c.1887–1915. The steep interior of Witches' Wood is not converted to pasture. See [Agriculture, 4-H and Rodeo](36-agriculture-ranching-4h-rodeo.md).
