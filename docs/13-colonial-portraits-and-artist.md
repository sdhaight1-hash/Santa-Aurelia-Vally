# The Founding Portraits and the Artist of Santa Aurelia

## Canon established by the user
**All four portraits** of the founders and their family are surviving works by **the same Spanish painter**, a **personal friend of Captain Alonso**, who **traveled with the founding colony in 1608** and became a famous artist. These are authentic **in-world colonial-period works**, not nineteenth-century pastiches. Their imagery forms part of the founding family’s archival heritage and genealogy.

**The couple ultimately had nine children.** The four painting subjects are: (1) the captain's arrival; (2) a later portrait of his wife Inés; (3) their wedding; and (4) a much later dynastic family portrait showing Alonso, Inés **and nine children**. The names, dates and individual identities of the nine offspring have not yet been decided; do not invent them as established family-tree people.

## Proposed painter: Maestro Mateo de Villacorta
*Working name, invented for this alternate-history setting, not an actual attributed artist.*

**Mateo de Villacorta** (working birth c.1580, death after c.1635) was already a respected and ambitious Spanish portraitist when he accompanied his longtime friend Alonso to the Columbia estuary aboard the 1608 founding expedition. His reputation later became considerable both in the colony and among Spanish patrons, carried by ships, commissions and copies of paintings. The colony gave him an unprecedented visual subject: its founder, the emerging Spanish–Indigenous household, the harbor, local landscapes and the daily life of the new settlement.

His social position is not merely a hired recorder: he was Alonso's **friend**, present throughout the early decades, acquainted with Inés, and trusted to make unusually intimate portraits rather than only stiff formal dynastic images. His works are foundational to the Santa Aurelian visual-art tradition, but paintings by him should not replace independent Indigenous cultural representations or imply that Spanish views are the only historical record.

Artist's exact full name, birthplace, chronology, eventual fame, studio, apprentices and the location of all original paintings remain **provisional**; the user only established a famous Spanish artist friend arriving with the founding colony.

## The four preserved works

### 1. *The Arrival of Don Alonso de Valcárcel y Austria*
**Subject:** Captain Alonso, his flagship, the Columbia estuary and the beginnings of the settlement. **Event:** 1608. **Painting:** proposed c.1608–1610, made shortly after arrival rather than literally while the ship docked. **Artist:** Mateo de Villacorta (working name). **Classification:** an original early colonial commemorative portrait.

### 2. *Portrait of Doña Inés of Santa Aurelia*
**Subject:** Inés in formal dress influenced by her own community and the developing colonial household. **Painting:** proposed later 1610s or 1620s. **Artist:** same friend and painter, with established personal familiarity. **Classification:** original colonial portrait. Her original Indigenous name and the precise provenance of jewelry and motifs are to be developed with care.

### 3. *The Marriage of Alonso and Inés*
**Subject:** the couple on their wedding day. **Historical event:** proposed 1611. **Painting:** proposed 1611–1613, whether done as contemporary sitting or shortly afterward. **Artist:** Mateo de Villacorta. **Classification:** original colonial wedding/union portrait, catalogued under **both spouse profiles** and their partnership.

### 4. *The Valcárcel Family at Santa Aurelia*
**Subject:** Alonso, Inés, and their **nine children**—**eleven people total**. **Painting:** substantially later, **proposed early–mid 1630s** after the births of all nine children and before Alonso's proposed death in 1641. The later date must remain compatible with the actual nine children's eventual birth dates. **Artist:** the same surviving founding-era Spanish painter, now an accomplished older master. **Classification:** original seventeenth-century family portrait, not an image assembled from descendants who could never have sat together.

## Preservation and provenance
All four paintings survive as artifacts in this fictional world's historical record through heirloom retention, changing residences, catalogues, restoration and later archival acquisition or loan. Proposed custodians include Valcárcel descendants, the Santa Aurelia maritime museum, civic collections or family foundations; **no definitive current ownership has been set**. Museum records should distinguish original paint, conservation treatments, frame replacements, historic copies and photographic reproductions.

The visual paintings generated during the conversation should be associated with these four catalog entries and linked to the family archive, once their image assets are uploaded to and served by the repository. **This document preserves the identity and metadata; it does not claim that image files have already been committed to GitHub.** Some architectural or heraldic details visible in the generated paintings may reflect artistic retrospective conventions or require historical refinement before final museum-catalog status.

## Established custody: the Valcárcel estate library
**User-confirmed:** the **main Valcárcel family line** has kept **all four original colonial paintings** and the **founding fleet's original logbooks** continuously in its private estate library/archive from the colonial era to the present. Earlier discussion of possible public museum ownership or dispersed family collections is superseded. Individual works may be temporarily conserved, copied, loaned or exhibited, but they are **not permanently transferred from the family collection**. See [The Valcárcel Estate Library](17-valcarcel-estate-library-and-archive.md). This does not imply the physical library room has remained unchanged for four centuries.

## The artist's surviving journals and tools
The family archive contains Villacorta's proposed personal sketchbooks, paintings studies and a surviving travel painter's chest, alongside the four original portraits. These are catalogued at [VA-JRN-001 and VA-OBJ-004](18-estate-library-collection-catalogue.md). The artist's journals are separate witnesses to the expedition and the private household; they should not be assumed to agree in every detail with official colony records.
