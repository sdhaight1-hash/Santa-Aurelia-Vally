# Valle de Santa Aurelia — Geographic Master Plan (Version 1, November 2026)

## Scope and status

**Adopted planning baseline:** The historically named Valle de Santa Aurelia is a geographically coherent **multi-watershed Oregon coastal and Coast Range region**, not a single flat valley or municipality. Its target area is **approximately 1,800 square miles (about 4,660 km²)**, with an irregular footprint and approximate **70-mile east–west / 45-mile north–south bounding scale**. The current draft polygon has a simple planar area estimate of approximately **1,863 sq mi**, only about 3% above the target; this is a **schematic cultural-region boundary**, not a legal parcel or surveyed watershed outline. Water/land percentages and precise sea-boundary allocation require GIS work.

**Anchor canon:** Santa Aurelia remains at the **real Astoria, Oregon** site. Willowwick Grove is placed along the **upper Youngs River corridor, southeast of Santa Aurelia**. Southbank is a separate township along a more downstream river reach. Blackwood Ridge and its Witches' Wood are in the **southern/southeastern uplands of the central valley**, with sovereign reservation lands overlapping its **northwestern side** and Fernhollow Country **southwest** of the ridge. Alderwood Lake belongs to an eastern upland basin. Exact legal land boundaries, topography and hydrologic grades have not been simulated yet.

All geographic coordinates below are **planning anchors for a fictional alternate-history landscape**, not claims that the fictional features exist at the same sites in our world. Real-world source landmarks are labeled separately.

## Coordinate reference and conventions

- Coordinate system: **WGS84 latitude / longitude**, decimal degrees, north-positive and east-positive (western longitudes negative).
- Planning geographic distances are approximate **road network distances**, not airline distances or verified routing.
- Status **fixed anchor** = user-approved real-world replacement locality (Santa Aurelia at Astoria). Status **proposed mapped** = explicit coordinates for design and later revision, now maintained in the location registry.
- Waterways should be derived from terrain elevation and watershed connectivity. Coordinates can indicate a corridor but cannot **guarantee a river reaches a proposed city** without a DEM and river-profile check.
- The 1,800-square-mile area is a **cultural-region target**, not a single hydrologic basin. Eastward headwaters and southern coastal tributaries may drain in different directions.

## The actual basin structure

### 1. Columbia estuary and coastal mouth
Santa Aurelia sits on the Columbia's south bank close to Youngs Bay. Ocean cruise berths and commercial shipping use dredged/deep-draft channels and pilotage; the Columbia Bar remains dangerous. The joint Navy–Coast Guard complex is outside the city, oriented toward Warrenton-area air and water facilities. The fort and outer-coast villages lie toward the river mouth and Pacific shore.

### 2. Youngs Bay–Youngs River system: primary civic spine
**The principal inland connection uses the Youngs River valley**, with Southbank on a downstream/middle reach and Willowwick farther upstream in the modified fictional upper basin. This is the simplest way to connect old river transport, industrial mills and a civic road to the port.

The **lower reaches near the estuary** can accommodate suitable launches and shallow commercial craft. **Do not assert a large historic paddlewheeler can reach Willowwick**; upstream grades, shoals, bends and water levels have to be modeled. Overland freight, road and historic rail make the city economical even if its upstream river is shallow.

### 3. Northern coast and Necanicum drainage
The beaches and settlements south of the Columbia mouth have a distinct coastal drainage, with separate outlet(s) to the Pacific. They should not be forced to drain into Youngs Bay. Garden estates, wedding venues, tide marshes, dunes and coastal tourism are plausible, but tsunami exposure is serious.

### 4. Eastern and southern watersheds
The upper Nehalem and other Coast Range tributaries and valleys enter the regional cultural boundary without all pointing toward Santa Aurelia. Alderwood Lake is a **new fictional basin requiring a plausible outlet, sediment history and seismic story**. The upper mining district and Blackwood slopes need terrain modeling to identify whether streams join Youngs, Nehalem, or other waterways.

### 5. Blackwood Ridge and Witches' Wood
A highland interior forest with relatively remote roads, hidden valleys and steep drainages. **Northwest overlap with sovereign reservation lands** and **southwestern transition to Fernhollow** are important directional requirements. The Fairy Stones and Silverveil Grove are sensitive sites with unsettled ownership and permission; mapping approximate reference points does not open the monuments to public tourism or assign title.

| Willowwick ↔ Alderwood Lake | 15–25 miles | ~30–50 minutes |

**These are targets, not live navigation results.** Youngs River roads should follow valleys and bridges; highland roads cross passes at actual feasible gradients; no fictional direct freeway through Witches' Wood. Connecting both big cities, Southbank and the joint base supports commuting, three-school sports rivalries, health services and a valleywide wedding and tourism economy.

### Proposed corridor system (conceptual)
- **Harbor–Valley Road:** primary improved road paralleling the Youngs River corridor from Santa Aurelia through Southbank toward Willowwick; partly follows adapted old valley routes, subject to steep grades and wetland protection.
- **Historic Mill Railway / multiuse corridor:** former resource-industry alignment with preserved station areas and selective active freight or trail sections. Rail history need not imply a present passenger railway.
- **South Coast Road:** connects Santa Aurelia to ocean-shore villages and the coastal recreation strip, respecting the watershed divides and real Highway 101 alignment.
- **Blackwood Mountain Road:** a winding access road to legal trailheads and inhabited margins only, not through sensitive sacred sites.
- **Lake Road:** Willowwick to the Alderwood Lake basin through eastern upland passes.

## Land cover and settlement scale

**Working aspirational proportions** of the cultural region: forests/protected uplands ~65%, farming/rural estates ~15%, settlements/infrastructure ~8%, inland waters/wetlands ~7%, open coastal/other ~5%. These are **visual design targets**; do not present as a remotely sensed classification or make a giant ocean area fit a terrestrial breakdown. A later GIS run should create exact area budgets excluding marine water when necessary.

Established population working values: Santa Aurelia ~19,000 and Willowwick ~21,000. Southbank's exact population, school enrollment and other settlement counts remain undecided. Scattered settlements, farms and forest conservation estates occupy most of the land. The concentration of two wealthy cities and a smaller ex-mill township does not imply contiguous suburban sprawl.

## Geological constraints and honest alternate-history divergence

Northern Oregon Coast Range relief is real, including volcanic and sedimentary belts, rugged forested slopes and river drainage. The user's fictional **ancient Aurelia cratonic fragment** with mineral deposits, and naturally appearing **Alderwood Lake basin**, require a **documented alternative geologic history** rather than treating ore or enormous lakes as typical real-world Astoria geology.

- **Gold district:** proposed ancient continental sliver exposed in parts of the upper valley (working hypothesis), hosting mineralized veins. Need real conceptual cross sections and rock/age stratigraphy.
- **Lake origin:** possible bedrock/structural basin with late-glacial or landslide modification, subject to hydrologic outlet and seismic stability. A large dammed lake should not be casually placed atop inhabited lowlands.
- **Youngs River:** test drainage continuity and water surface; do not route uphill into Willowwick without a survey.
- **Cascadia seismic/tsunami risk:** protect downtown port but do not invent immunity. Consider buildable terraces, deep foundation conditions and recovery after 1700.
- **Saddle Mountain:** keep distinct from Blackwood Ridge unless later alternate tectonic mapping deliberately moves or modifies its contours.

## Prototype region outline

The polygon stored in **atlas/geography.geojson** is a **schematic study boundary** with no legal standing. It reaches the Columbia mouth, southern beaches, interior Coast Range, and the upland edge farther east. Its calculated area uses a flat local approximation; **actual geodesic GIS area must be recomputed** before any precision claims. Jurisdictions and sensitive tribal land holdings cannot be inferred from that polygon.

## Natural-history refinement: basalt coastline, gorge and moorland
This plan is supplemented by [Natural History of Valle de Santa Aurelia](30-natural-history-geology-climate-ecology.md). Four **provisional** markers are added to [atlas/geography.geojson](../atlas/geography.geojson): **Black Steps** (45.950, -123.980), polygonal basalt rocky coast on an outer-volcanic headland; **Aurelia High Gorge** (45.970, -123.590), a proposed 8–15-mile scenic upper inland canyon with cliffs and tributary waterfalls; **Blackwood Ravines** (45.918, -123.655), smaller dark, humid creek gorges; and **Open Moor Ridges** (45.910, -123.704), local windswept grassy/heath patches giving a Scottish-Highlands-like silhouette in Oregon's Coast Range. Preserve the old-growth heart of Witches' Wood. **Do not imply these sites exist in real Oregon or that exact elevations, river courses, tidal accessibility and watershed membership have been verified.** These planned features require a geologically credible uplift/erosion sequence and Coast Range drainage checks, not a direct import of Missoula flood formation or of Scotland's vegetation.

## Naturalized wildlife distributions (range hypotheses)
Two additional **fictional naturalized species** inhabit the region: **sika deer** introduced in working c.1880s at a Willowwick-related estate (South Hills and outer Blackwood/woodland lake country), and **red-necked wallabies** escaped in working c.1905 from a Fernhollow naturalist's enclosure (Fernhollow and nearby sheltered glens, scattered lake margins). These are **broad ecological ranges, not exact sightings or habitat polygons**; do not replace protected native habitat with exotic animal parks. Their introduction sites, density, life history and ecological implications are detailed in [Naturalized Exotic Wildlife](31-naturalized-exotic-wildlife.md). The GeoJSON registry gains no false-precision animal colony point locations at this stage.
