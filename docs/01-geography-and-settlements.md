# Geography and settlement pattern

## Geographic premise
**Valle de Santa Aurelia** is the historical and cultural name for a connected lower Columbia–northern Oregon Coast Range region rather than necessarily one literal enclosed valley. It spans estuary, maritime coast, river bottoms, forested foothills, rugged uplands and inland valleys. The original Indigenous names of the individual waterways and homelands have not yet been decided; do not fabricate purported Chinookan words.

**Santa Aurelia** occupies the **actual site of Astoria, Oregon**, on the south shore of the Columbia near its Pacific estuary. The imagined 1608 colony and subsequent city replace Astoria's actual post-1811 city history. Protected anchorage is plausible only with some local harbor/geography divergence or prudent choice of sheltered sites; the Columbia bar remains a serious hazard. River shoreline, Youngs Bay, Youngs River and nearby estuarine landforms are grounded in that real geography unless deliberately adjusted.

**Willowwick Grove** is a separate inland city in the Coast Range watershed, founded as a mining settlement, subsequently the Garden City of the Lower Columbia. **Precise location not fixed**: locate it on a navigable or historically useful inland river corridor with mining foothills, agricultural bottoms, forests, and realistic road travel to Santa Aurelia. The town has both ornamental and natural deciduous tree cover, making autumn color and spring blossoms outstanding.

**Blackwood Ridge** is a more remote upland district containing **Witches' Wood**. The larger woodland spans jurisdictional boundaries. **Northwest:** sovereign reservation territory extends into the forest itself. **Southwest:** Fernhollow Country, old immigrant settlements and prepper/homestead country occupy outer margins. The Fairy Stones and Silverveil Grove lie in the central ancient wood, potentially near an as-yet-unresolved reservation/protected-forest boundary. Do not assume the primary monument's land title.

## Conceptual, not surveyed, directional layout
The Columbia estuary and Santa Aurelia anchor the coastal/northern approach. Willowwick is inland; Blackwood Ridge sits within the surrounding uplands. Within Blackwood Ridge, the reservation and a portion of Witches' Wood overlap toward the northwest; Fernhollow and surrounding rural settlements lie southwest. Adjacent other valleys connect to smaller mining, timber, lake and agricultural communities. **Relative directions are fixed; coordinates and distances are not.**

## Regional districts and named places
**Coastal Reach:** historic Santa Aurelia waterfront, protected river frontage, Columbia mouth, working port, fishing towns, heritage coast and an old defense fort (the Fort Stevens/Hammond vicinity provides a useful real geographic counterpart). Marine weather, storm exposure, and estuary ecology matter.

**Lower Valley:** river commerce, floodplain farms, orchards, small settlements, former industrial waterfronts, river bridges, wetlands and willow stands. Vulnerable to flooding and sedimentation; historic trade corridors survive in the modern road network.

**Upper Valley:** waterfalls, steep tributaries, whitewater, historic mining and logging ground, mountain trails, forest roads, and challenging winter storms.

**South Hills:** rural farms, family estates, orchards, ranching, cabins, hunters, bow-makers, archery and self-reliant homesteads.

**Alderwood Lake Country:** recreational lake, canoeing, historic timber-era **Alderwood Lodge** resort, camping and lake settlements. Its precise geology and road linkage remain to be mapped.

**Blackwood Ridge:** Witches' Wood, forest traditions, reservation lands toward northwest, Fernhollow district to southwest, old immigrant settlements, archaeological stoneways, rare endemic groves, trails and mines.

**Fernhollow Country:** broader southwest rural district, not merely the ecovillage. Contains old farms and immigrant households, ecological education, independent homesteads, and nearby **Thornhaven Village**.

## Conservation and land tenure
Different forest parcels can be reservation land under sovereign tribal authority; state or local managed forest; public recreation land; privately owned timber, farms and conservation easements; and specially protected ecological/archaeological habitat. A named forest is **not a single legal jurisdiction**. The tribe's authority over reservation parcels is real and independent; joint management for adjacent protected sites requires genuine agreements, not a fiction of automatically shared sovereignty.

## Revised socioeconomic settlement boundaries
**Both Willowwick Grove and Santa Aurelia are broadly affluent, maintained and commercially healthy cities, without major designated impoverished districts.** Their populations still include ordinary wage-earners, renters and households under pressure. More economically distressed former mine, mill and logging communities lie in **separate townships and unincorporated places** across the lower and upper valley. Do not map them as districts of either city; each has distinct boundaries, services and civic identity. The old Willowwick **Southbank Flats** concept is split: a restored in-city **Millwright Quarter**, and **Southbank**, an independent less-affluent township outside city limits. [Regional economic geography](27-prosperity-and-outlying-townships.md).

## Geographic master plan v1 — adopted planning map
The established **Valle de Santa Aurelia cultural region** has a **roughly 1,800 sq mi / 4,660 km²** working footprint, with a schematic irregular boundary roughly 70 miles east–west by 45 miles north–south. See [Geographic Master Plan](29-geographic-master-plan.md) and the machine-readable [GeoJSON study registry](../atlas/geography.geojson). **Santa Aurelia** retains the Astoria site at approximately 46.1880, -123.8313; **Willowwick Grove** is now provisionally positioned at 46.0100, -123.6650 in the upper Youngs River corridor; **Southbank** at 46.0650, -123.7380 as an independent middle-valley township. The Blackwood highlands and Witches' Wood are south of Willowwick, Fernhollow southwest of Blackwood, reservation overlap to the ridge's northwest, Alderwood Lake in an eastern upland basin. These are **planning point centers, not surveyed land or tribal jurisdiction boundaries**, and must be validated against future hydrology/terrain maps. Do not turn this historically named region into one drainage basin or infer that paddlewheelers can navigate the upper river.

## Integrated natural history — Highland-like ridges, basalt shore, great gorge
**Established setting direction:** Valle de Santa Aurelia remains fundamentally the Oregon Coast Range and Columbia estuary, but includes **localized Scottish-Highland-like open grassy/heath-covered ridges and misty glens**, a **Giant's Causeway-like exposed columnar basalt shore** on the outer Pacific coast, and **one major upper-valley Columbia Gorge-like incised river canyon** plus smaller, shaded **Blackwood ravines**. These are distinct geological zones, not one continuous valley. Proposed names and locations: **Black Steps** basalt beach (45.950, -123.980), **Aurelia High Gorge** (45.970, -123.590), **Blackwood Ravines** (45.918, -123.655), **Open Moor Ridges** (45.910, -123.704). Preserve native Oregon ecology, rainforest, Youngs Bay estuary, multiple watershed divides and the unknown geology/outlet of Alderwood Lake. The historical **cratonic fragment** remains fictional and requires a coherent tectonic explanation. Coordinates are planning anchors subject to future DEM/shoreline validation; source references and ecological detail in [Natural History of the Valley](30-natural-history-geology-climate-ecology.md).
