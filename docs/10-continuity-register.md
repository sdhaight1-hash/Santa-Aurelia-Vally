## Explicit later revisions that supersede earlier drafts
**Regional title:** replace Willowwick-centric setting framing with **Valle de Santa Aurelia** as the region; keep **Willowwick Grove** as inland city, and **Santa Aurelia** as coastal city.

**European founding:** supersede earlier proposed **1853**, **1810**, and **1772** colonial start dates. **1608** is the first European colonial permanent settlement date. Indigenous residence long predates it.

**Founding place:** Santa Aurelia is specifically **at real-world Astoria**; not an invented remote unnamed coastal harbor. This necessarily changes John Jacob Astor/Fort Astoria's real 1811 narrative.

**Earthquake:** earlier drafts made the 1700 Cascadia disaster effectively relocate/recreate the port inland. Revised canon: **Santa Aurelia rebuilds and remains substantial**, while an inland settlement corridor also expands.

**Population:** Santa Aurelia is nearly Willowwick Grove's equal, **~19,000 vs ~21,000** working figure, greater region ~60,000; not a minor village.

**Old-money origins:** eastern American Connecticut/Virginia migrations and Ashcroft/Blackthorn-related fortunes are **primarily Willowwick**. Santa Aurelia's elite comes mainly from older Spanish-Indigenous maritime families and **later Venetian investment**.

**Architecture:** Santa Aurelia distinctive Spanish–Indigenous **and later Venetian** regional style; Willowwick primarily Victorian/Gothic/garden-city. Do not transfer architectural histories indiscriminately.

**Alonso and Inés:** the union is **mutual passionate love**, not a marriage primarily arranged for diplomacy. It also yields important political kinship consequences. Their descendants survive across social classes.

**Ball:** seventeenth-century formal assembly and ball-hall, progressively expanded; modern debutante forms and romantic festival dedication arrived later. Do not portray the ball as founded in Victorian era.

**Forest names:** **Fairy Stones** is preferred common name of stone circle in luminous Silverveil Grove; **Giants' Ring** remains myth-linked alternative; Witches' Wood contains both the stones and endemic giant Blacksilver Willows.

**Forest geography:** reservation **includes northwest portions of Witches' Wood**, not merely adjacent; **Fernhollow southwest** and dispersed prepper/homestead communities; the main archaeological site has not been definitively assigned to reservation or non-reservation land.

## Sáweli/Inés: major historian, not solely founder's wife (later explicit canon)
**Established:** After learning alphabetic literacy as an adult, Sáweli/Inés became an **avid and accomplished writer, historian, oral-history interviewer and chronicler**. Her substantial surviving writings describe her Ashalewan childhood before 1608 **retrospectively**, and—most consequentially—record **conversations with people from several distinct Indigenous nations**, especially Blackwood Ridge storytellers about the **White Ones, ancient stones, waystones, lights and other forest legends**. The Blackwood folklore is therefore **attested by early seventeenth-century written primary-source interviews as well as living oral traditions**. Such documentation establishes that the stories were circulating, not the literal existence of giants/spirits. Originals remain in the Valcárcel family's private library. The eight proposed archive series and exact dates are working reconstructions. [Monograph](19-saweli-ines-historian-and-oral-histories.md).

## The assembly hall is Alonso's gift to Inés — explicit revision
**Established:** Alonso built the **original Casa de las Asambleas as a gift to Sáweli/Inés**, who had fallen in love with the beauty and excitement of royal and aristocratic gatherings encountered during their journeys to Spain. She influenced the hall's design and social purpose. Preserve working completion **1638**, possible inaugural celebration **1638–1639**, and later expansions to **Palacio de la Bahía**. **Do not rewrite the hall as a pre-1608 building or invent the formal annual assembly in 1638**: annual social gatherings already existed in the early colony and modern debutante practices appeared considerably later. The gift explains the historic *building*, not the origin of all festivities. See [gift and architectural history](21-casa-de-las-asambleas-love-gift.md).

## The hall was wildly impractical by design — explicit latest revision
Alonso's lifelong character is **practical and financially disciplined**, making the Casa de las Asambleas exceptional. **He knowingly spent a fortune of his own money** giving Sáweli/Inés a wildly extravagant ball and assembly palace. Timber framing was an initial construction phase, **not the final architectural ambition**: the original became a monumental **Spanish stone-and-masonry palace with elegant details during their own lives**. Do not justify the building primarily as a prudent investment or portray its restrained wooden phase as the lasting original. Respect the proposed **1641** death: Alonso's personally directed extravagance belongs c.1635–1641, not to the later 1640s, unless his lifespan is retconned. The exact **1638** hall dedication remains working, with additional masonry work to c.1641 and descendant-led subsequent expansions. See [hall history](21-casa-de-las-asambleas-love-gift.md).

## Festival's multi-hall social circuit and Venetian hall rivalry (explicit latest canon)
**Established:** later Venetian-descended family estates built their own **Venetian-style grand ballrooms**, some reaching or exceeding the original Spanish Casa de las Asambleas in magnificence. Santa Aurelia experienced a sustained era of wealthy families **competing to create the grandest halls**, giving the city a remarkable concentration of palatial gathering spaces. The modern **Festival of the Two Hearts and debutante ball season extend through many regional halls**, but the **Palacio de la Bahía remains the historic ceremonial seat and the site of the culminating Grand Ball** because of Alonso's extravagant gift to Sáweli/Inés. Do not imply all balls happen at one venue, that the Venetians built the first grand Spanish palace, or that a later rival displaced the Palacio's historic status. Working: names Bellafonte, Marovelli, Contarini-Valdesi; c.1850–1910 rivalry and detailed event progression. [The Season of Halls](22-season-of-halls-venetian-ballrooms.md).

## Witches' Wood old growth, edible abundance and Fernhollow ecological research
**Established by user:** attempts by large timber companies to harvest the most ancient Witches' Wood tracts repeatedly ended in costly failure, disaster or abandonment, contributing to extensive surviving undisturbed old-growth forest. This does **not** negate successful logging elsewhere in the valley or prove an objectively supernatural forest defense. The ancient forest is also a **well-known foraging landscape with many wild edible resources** in appropriate habitats and access-permitted areas; it is not universally hostile to visitors or universally edible. **Fernhollow ecovillage has devoted decades to substantial ecological research of Witches' Wood and uses that knowledge as the foundation for its practical food-forest plans.** It observes forest structure, fungi, soils, native understory, seasonal foraging and hydrology without assuming ownership or access rights in tribal/protected areas. Cultivated food forests remain distinct from ancient old growth. See [doc 33](33-fernhollow-food-forest-ecology.md) and [doc 07](07-blackwood-communities.md).

## Blacksilver Willow ebony heartwood — latest user-established material canon
**Blacksilver Willow** (native/endemic, especially Silverveil Grove) is prized not only for its giant cascading silver foliage and charcoal bark but for **rich near-ebony heartwood**. Local craftspeople strongly value selected wood for **traditional bows** owing to surprisingly favorable bow-making behavior, and for **high-quality furniture, fine joinery, inlays, carved details and architectural decoration**. These unusual properties belong to the *fictional* species, not ordinary real-world willow. This does **not** override ancient-grove protection, Indigenous land authority or the history of catastrophic industrial logging attempts. **Latest user correction supersedes earlier supply speculation:** Blacksilver has been cultivated from cuttings across the valley **for centuries**. Such trees generally have a **lower mature growth maximum** than ancient Witches' Wood specimens, yet supply **most wood used for bows, fine furniture and architecture**, rather than harvesting from ancient forest. Exact anatomy, cause of smaller size, yields and lineage details remain open. See [Blacksilver Willow Woodcraft](34-blacksilver-willow-woodcraft.md).

## Witches' Wood conservation status — latest user-established revision
**Much of Witches' Wood remains old-growth because legend, taboo, repeated logging disasters and reluctance to disturb the deep forest have acted as powerful informal protections, even without one comprehensive legally recognized designation.** People do still forage or conduct research on appropriate accessible parcels, so it is not universally forbidden. **Fernhollow ecovillage and the sovereign tribal government are actively working to secure formal comprehensive protection.** They have overlapping conservation interests but independent authority; existing tribal sovereignty and any parcel-specific legal protections continue to apply. Do not describe the whole forest as already designated a national park, legally unprotected in every parcel, or privately owned by Fernhollow.
